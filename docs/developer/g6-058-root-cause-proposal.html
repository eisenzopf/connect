2. Root causes and confidence

ConfirmedR1 — Core commits before an ordinary Service policy exists

At the diagnosed full-workload baseline, the cutover sequence was:

  1. Core commits the managed replacement and retires the old controller.
  2. The provisional TargetPolicy::scope refuses the target once handle.is_committed() is true. This correctly prevents preparation authority from becoming ordinary call authority.
  3. Service records the physical capture, projects established Store state, creates the Data writer, reacquires adoption authority and confirms the managed audience.
  4. adopt_replacement inserts the ordinary connection entry and removes the provisional target.

Between steps 1 and 4, the old physical route is gone and the provisional path rejects the new one. The 499.65 ms interval above is therefore a real lifecycle gap, not merely slow status polling. Ordinary packets can be refused throughout it.

There is also a public-state ordering gap: Store sets the candidate ready, marks the operation completed and journals connection_ready before Data writer creation and Service entry adoption. In one v26 move the signed completion observation precedes adoption by about 31 ms. Readiness publication should describe an installed ordinary route, while keeping decoded video readiness distinct from a muted negotiated stream.

A simple flag reorder is unsafe. Replacement-origin admission currently requires operations.state='completed'. That origin is needed by Data writer construction and adoption. Delaying the flag without a new private preparation/activation path would create a dependency cycle. Removing the provisional committed check would incorrectly lend preparation authority to ordinary traffic.

Confirmed blocking; live amplification strongly supportedR2 — Retained source fallback still parks the mixer

The earlier scheduling fix covers Receive callbacks and callbacks with an active weak source-custody context. When that weak context has expired, a retained source still needs a fresh current authorization read. Core falls back to MediaPolicy::current; Service classifies Publish as fresh ingress and retries leaf metadata/Store contention synchronously for up to 10 ms.

The QUIC driver does poll its media reader alongside signaling, and the physical peer pumps media while signed controls are pending. The source audit therefore does not support a blanket claim that every pending command stops datagram polling. Synchronous retry inside a packet callback can still occupy the cooperative runtime worker; low average OS CPU is not proof that that worker is available.

The actual mixer can already own another source's current reader while admitting the next source. Captured recording needs complete Conversation/identity writer scopes. Its fair acquisition budget is 5 ms, followed by a 20 ms metadata retry after refusal. A 10 ms callback can keep an existing reader alive beyond that writer's budget; repeated retries amplify the gap. Merely increasing the writer timeout could instead block more fresh media.

The newly completed actual Service diagnostic measures 160.01854 ms for 16 retained-source callbacks under a held leaf registry. It fails its 50 ms occupancy limit with 78 other functions filtered out; executable, source and prepared inputs remain unchanged. This proves callback blocking. A controlled mixer/Store writer-drain case and low-volume retry reasons are still needed to assign the entire live 449 ms to this mechanism rather than SQL or other contention.

Confirmed; fixed in v26R3 — Temporary video fanout refusal used to discard the packet

The scene previously removed every realtime destination after try_forward_to returned an error, including temporary NotReady. That loses compressed references before the destination egress queue, so zero full-queue counts did not reveal it. The implemented fix retains only temporary refusal for a later tick, keeping the exact packet, receiver port/ticket and earliest observed authorization deadline through queued delivery.

Five new focused cases pass, including recovery, original age/expiry, revocation/subscription changes, receiver replacement and queued expiry. This defect is closed at component scope. Keep the correction; do not treat its substantial video improvement as closure of R1, R2 or startup.

Confirmed design and observed delayR4 — Video activation and decoder bootstrap happen too late

The physical peer waits for the original signed completed root. It then performs two separately durable/current-authorized commands: video/Data unmute and incoming stream subscription. The media pump sets video_open=true only after both return. The first outgoing frame must then be a keyframe from the original preencoded programme. A new receiver also joins the retained sender's existing dependency chain.

On route 0's latest return, ownership observation is at 42.601 s, the first received video row is at 44.375 s, and the first decodable keyframe is at 45.020 s. The stopped journal independently places subscription completion in its coarse 44.155–45.155 s programme-time bucket; timestamps have one-second resolution and cannot identify subphase duration more precisely. This is additional delay beyond the audio activation gap.

The G6 fixture uses a 500 kbit/s target with up to a 30-frame/one-second GOP. The separate native-video example uses a six-frame/200 ms GOP and a different 1 Mbit/s target because native QUIC has no negotiated keyframe feedback. Both choices satisfy the contract's maximum-one-second keyframe interval, but they have different recovery behavior. The fixture cannot guarantee strict every-second frame coverage by waiting for a periodic keyframe after late subscription.

The native-video example drops dependent interframes after a sequence hole until a fresh keyframe. The independent moving verifier instead retains received frames in its per-history decode and reports corrupted marker pixels. Production playout may therefore suppress damaged frames rather than display them; that still leaves a coverage/recovery failure. Changing only the verifier to suppress bad pixels would not supply missing frames or fix bootstrap.

Do not hide this by using all-intra video, resetting the source clock, sharing decoder state between devices, or replaying stale bootstrap frames. The fix needs explicit authorized video-transfer preparation and fresh decoder bootstrap. Request-driven fresh keyframes are preferable when retaining the current strict continuity requirement.

Code-confirmed loss paths; contribution not yet quantifiedR5 — Other temporary refusals can still become permanent loss

  • Audio receiver admission: the mixer drains source PCM into the tick's segments before checking each receiver. A temporary target NotReady skips that segment. Existing source collect/take recovery does not preserve that receiver's consumed quantum. A focused case is drafted but has not run.
  • Native ingress: PortIngress maps every non-Backpressure error, including temporary NotReady, to a closed/refused result. The physical reader treats it as rejected rather than owning a bounded deferred attempt. These losses happen before the queue observations.
  • Partial VP8 input: admission is per RTP packet. A temporary refusal during a fragmented keyframe can prevent a complete keyframe from being assembled. Saved holes do not yet identify whether this or another boundary lost each remaining packet.

These paths need precise counters and controlled cases before choosing a buffering design. Re-inserting PCM globally would duplicate audio at receivers already served. Re-admitting a deferred frame against a newer audience would violate the original privacy boundary.

3. Measurement and task-scope issues

The current decoder cut is a polling observation

transport_moving_av.timeline cuts ownership at the physical peer's observed completed-root time and forbids earlier rows from initializing its decoder. This protects against using private preparation traffic, but observed completion is not the actual activation epoch. In v25 one root observation lagged adoption by about 134 ms; in the worst v26 return the lag is only about 15 ms. It can distort some coverage, but cannot explain the latest 499 ms policy gap or retained-route RTP losses.

Use an authenticated activation/stream-epoch marker tied to the exact original operation, connection incarnation, stream mapping and first ordinary media. Keep captured rows in their original time coordinates and classify them once the marker is known. A host debug timestamp alone is not sufficient authority to relabel preparation packets.

The moving-workload acceptance profile needs an explicit definition

The frozen contract has clean 24 fps/60 s coverage, 200 ms media-age bounds and a separate one-second device-interruption limit. The moving workload additionally requires 59.9 seconds of decoded audio, maximum audio arrival gap 200 ms, at least 24 video frames in every second, and zero bad markers across all four moves. The 59.9-second and arrival-gap thresholds are in the validator rather than explicitly enumerated in the matrix contract.

Those conditions form a stricter composite than the standalone one-second handoff allowance. They are not logically impossible, but the current late-control/periodic-keyframe lifecycle does not guarantee them. This proposal preserves all current thresholds. Document clean continuity, interruption, queue age, arrival jitter, content loss and decoder bootstrap as separate measurements. Any later threshold/profile change would require an explicit reviewed amendment and new identified evidence.

The task requires 32 cells: two server platforms × two local/remote-fixture profiles × four transports × two signing cohorts. P2 implementation shape and boundaries

The proposed internal state sequence is private prepared → physical committed/captured → ordinary entry installed but inactive → durable Ready/Completed → ordinary current admission. The private context binds the original root, attempt/effect, runtime, candidate/retained/source incarnations, current credentials, negotiated resources and generation. It is usable to prepare/adopt metadata only; it is not a MediaAuthorityLease and cannot publish, receive ordinary media or authorize a historical replay.

Video preservation must be explicitly authorized by the replacement/media-transfer intent. Preparation keeps the candidate isolated and must not automatically unmute a camera the caller intended to keep muted. A precisely attributed, known activation-pending state can be retryable; revocation, wrong generation and uncertain/unattributed effects remain refusals. This is not permission to convert arbitrary Authority errors into retries.

The final Store transition enables normal current-origin checks only after the inactive entry and observer exist. This removes the current constructor dependency on prematurely public completion. The design must preserve the existing lock-order rule: release the role coordinator before waiting for an authority writer; hold no SQL mutex across native/network awaits. Do not return a writer permit and then wait asynchronously for a coordinator needed by an admitted reader.

Use explicit publication/activation guards for the short final transition, not a cached permission snapshot. Keep capture projection idempotent.
