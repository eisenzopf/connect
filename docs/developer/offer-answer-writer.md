# Answers during native offer preparation

`AuthorizedEnvelope::answer_offer` writes one exact `connection.answer` while the handler continues preparing the offer. The same authenticated physical actor owns signaling, signing, queue bounds and writer progress. This is the response step required before waiting for media observations. An answer never establishes media readiness, Participant presence or a durable operation receipt.

The source must be an ongoing `connection.offer` with exact Conversation, Session and Connection scope and a stable logical operation ID. The answer must retain those scopes and the original logical operation ID and correlate `in_reply_to` to the actual client offer. The actor assigns a fresh wire ID/timestamp and signature. Arbitrary events, another request's scope, or readiness disguised as an answer are refused before submission.

`CurrentAuthority::negotiation_answer` and its bound `PeerAuthority` hook explicitly approve the negotiated participant, resource, capabilities and bindings using the same current invocation guard. The default refuses. These synchronous hooks must not await or reacquire their grant/key-store locks. Ordinary command permission and the separate exchange-request hook do not authorize an answer.

One invocation can claim one answer. Concurrent attempts cannot enqueue duplicate answers, and dispatch rejects a repeated final answer after an in-handler submission. A refused call mailbox or physical writer queue reports capacity and permits retry on that same invocation before any IO. No automatic retry occurs after IO may have begun. Preserve the logical operation ID and reconcile uncertainty through the application's existing journal.

Pending work owns a cancellable slot for its actual authority lease, rather than independent readers in queue copies. Producer loss releases a pending reader promptly. Only the physical writer claims the lease at its dequeue. Its sink/IO custody destroys sink-owned pending IO before releasing that reader, including whole actor-future abortion. A stale item before IO leaves a healthy peer usable. Possibly partial IO reports `SubmissionUnknown` to the local completion channel and retires that writer. Retirement can abort the containing handler before it polls its completion; no caller completion or durable outcome is inferred from that abortion.

After an actual successful write, `finish_answered_offer` produces a private empty completion tied to this exact invocation. Dispatch revalidates both the identity and observed write. This lets the handler finish without a fabricated final ACK. Anonymous empty batches, completion before writing, another invocation's proof and saved contexts after handler return cannot authorize that path. Existing ordinary reply batches remain nonempty. Resumed step-up work preserves its independent authentication-transition response.

The source-bound local macOS arm64 runner is `scripts/check-gate6-offer-answer.py`. Its selected cases exercise actual signed QUIC/WebTransport/WSS answers, full scope/correlation denial and recovery, concurrent/final duplicate refusal, same-held authority behind a queued writer, default-policy refusal, saved-context lifetime, actual QUIC pending and partial-write cancellation, full writer-queue refusal and same-offer recovery without late cancelled RPC IO, and sink-before-reader destruction on abort/deadline. These are answer scheduling and custody checks with controlled policy/codec fixtures.

Public capability selection, complete offer/answer engine orchestration, real per-binding media-flow observations, `connection.ready`, `stream.opened`/`stream.closed`, confirmation/presence and general subscription handlers remain required G6-020 work. The protocol defines ready as a server media observation, not a client ACK. Production Connect Session/message/journal and Store-policy installation, automatic credential refresh/step-up integration, other guarded transports and the remaining Gate 6 tasks remain open. This component does not qualify those behaviors or close Gate 6 acceptance.
